What I read: 31 stories, the 5 epics and `decision-blocker:m0-github-repository`, via `aep plan artifact list`, `relations`, `waves` and `validate` (valid, 45 artifacts) and a full read of every story body. Surfaces: all 31 bodies name paths (step 1, cited), none of the files exist yet, and the 8 M2 stories also carry recorded `scope`. The 23 listed above have no recorded `scope`. Unplaceable: 0 by body, 1 with a wrong crate (m3-one-vocabulary-across-sources). Acceptance reads traced to a producer in the set: 24; 23 are recorded by `depends_on` (18 to the producer story, 5 to its epic only: m1-instance-registry and m1-eventlog-store on the M0 stories); 1 is not (m2-hysteresis-core).

What I could not establish:
- Whether a story's `depends_on` on an epic orders it after all of that epic's stories. `waves` prints only story-to-story ordering, and the 5 M1-on-M0 reads above rest on it. If it does not order them, the M1 stories also collide with story:m0-workspace on `Cargo.toml` and `Cargo.lock`.
- The M3 adapter stories share `crates/thalamus-sources/src/lib.rs` openly ("shared with every M3 adapter story"). That is a named, deliberate collision, so I raised no finding on it.
- Out of my lane, which did not set the verdict:
  - The M1 CLI keeps `eval.rs` and `show.rs` flat, while M2 uses `src/cmd/*.rs`.
  - The scheduler in `thalamus-service` must reuse the grading path that story:m1-cli-eval-show puts in the `thalamus-cli` binary crate, with no shared library planned (design critic).
  - m0-ci's acceptance waits on an open decision blocker, which is a status matter, not mine.
